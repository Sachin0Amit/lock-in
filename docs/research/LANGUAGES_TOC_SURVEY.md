# The framework anchor — CEFR, and the exam registers

## The Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR)

The Council of Europe's official level-description page read this pass
(coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/
level-description): **six levels A1 to C2, regrouped into three broad bands —
Basic User (A1, A2), Independent User (B1, B2), Proficient User (C1, C2)**,
with the can-do descriptors per skill. The CEFR is the shelf's common
spine: every European-route book (German, French, Spanish, Italian,
Portuguese, Russian as a European-language reading; English) prices its
stages against it, and the non-European registers (JLPT, HSK, TOPIK) are
mapped to it honestly at their recognised equivalences.

## The official exam registers (each read this pass)

- **JLPT — Japanese-Language Proficiency Test** (jlpt.jp, the official site):
  **five levels N1, N2, N3, N4 and N5 — the easiest N5, the hardest N1, N3 the
  bridging level between N1/N2 and N4/N5**; the test-sections page
  (jlpt.jp/e/guideline/testsections.html) read this pass — Language Knowledge
  (Vocabulary) + Language Knowledge (Grammar)・Reading + Listening as the
  three test sections with per-level times. The japanese book's spine:
  N5 → N4 → (N3 named as the bridge).
- **Goethe-Zertifikat — German** (goethe.de, the official exam pages read
  this pass): the Goethe-Institut runs **German exams for all levels of
  ability, ranging A1 to C2** (the SD1/SdA family at A1, the B1/B2/C1/C2
  Zertifikat ladder). The german book's spine: A1 → B1, the ladder named.
- **DELF / DALF — French** (the official diploma pages read this pass —
  lallianceny.org/exams/delf-dalf + the DELF Wikipedia record): official
  qualifications awarded by the French Ministry of Education for non-native
  speakers — **DELF A1 and A2 for basic users, DELF B1 and B2 for independent
  users, DALF C1 and C2 for proficient users**. The french book's spine:
  DELF A1 → DELF B1 (DALF named).
- **DELE — Diplomas de Español** (Instituto Cervantes pages read this pass —
  cervantes.org/us/los-angeles + the London centre's exam-format page +
  dele.org): **six exam levels A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2 established by the
  CEFR**; the B1 format read in detail (reading comprehension 70 min,
  listening 40 min, written expression and interaction 60 min, oral
  expression and interaction 30 min — the four-skill structure). The spanish
  book's spine: DELE A1 → DELE B1.
- **HSK — Hanyu Shuiping Kaoshi** (chinesetest.cn, the official Chinese
  Test Service site, read this pass): the **HSK test syllabus covers five
  components — tasks, topics, vocabulary, grammar and Chinese characters** —
  and the registration page confirms **HSK Level 5 and Level 6 tests**
  register separately (the current HSK 2.0 register of levels 1–6); the
  New HSK 3.0 (2021/2026, levels 1–9 with the advanced band) read via the
  khanjischool.com analysis (level-3 vocabulary ~1,000 cumulative-style
  counts). The mandarin book's spine: HSK 1–4 register, HSK 3.0 named
  honestly as the evolving standard.
- **TOPIK — Test of Proficiency in Korean** (topik.go.kr, the official NIIED
  site, read this pass): **TOPIK I and TOPIK II** — TOPIK II
  (intermediate-advanced) covering **sublevels 3, 4, 5 and 6** (the topikguide
  register read this pass). The korean book's spine: TOPIK I (levels 1–2),
  TOPIK II named.
- **IELTS — International English Language Testing System** (ielts.org, the
  official scoring page read this pass): **the band scale 0–9 — 9 Expert,
  8 Very good, 7 Good, 6 Competent, 5 Modest, 4 Limited** …; the four skills
  (Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking) with the band score per skill and
  overall (takeielts.britishcouncil.org read this pass). The english book's
  exam spine.
- **TOEFL iBT** (ets.org/toefl test-takers content page read this pass):
  **four sections — Reading, Listening, Speaking and Writing**; the 2026
  update's two-stage adaptive Reading/Listening noted honestly.
- **CLEP Spanish Language Levels 1 and 2** (clep.collegeboard.org read this
  pass): measures skills typically acquired through **two to four semesters**
  of study; the Spanish-with-Writing variant's structure (100 multiple-choice
  + two writing tasks) noted. The spanish book's North-American register.
- No official single world register exists for Hindi, Sanskrit, Arabic,
  Portuguese, Italian or Russian at the same tier; the honest spines used:
  the Indian school register (NCERT Hindi/Sanskrit), the ACTFL-style
  proficiency ladder named for all, the Al-Kitaab Arabic arc's own level
  progression, the CAPLE/CEFR Portuguese register and the CILS/CELR Italian
  registers named, and the TORFL Russian state test named.
