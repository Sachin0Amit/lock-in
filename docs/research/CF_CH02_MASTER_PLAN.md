## The 2.3 arc (the cf23 agent)
The chapter's two device lessons built both halves of the handshake; 2.3
asks the QUALITY question — how well does the conversation go? Sections:
(1) what 'easy to use' actually means (usability defined; the instinct
census: learnability, efficiency, error-tolerance, satisfaction —
qualitative); (2) the lineage of 'easy' — Bush's Memex (Atlantic, July
1945) → Sketchpad (Sutherland, 1963, TX-2 light pen) → Engelbart's 9 Dec
1968 demo (banked in 2.1) → Xerox Alto (1 Mar 1973, 606×808, never sold)
→ Xerox Star 8010 (27 Apr 1981, $16,595) → Apple Lisa (19 Jan 1983,
$9,995) → Macintosh 128K (24 Jan 1984, $2,495, the '1984' Super Bowl XVIII
ad) → Windows 1.0 (20 Nov 1985); (3) WIMP + the desktop metaphor (power
and honest limits); (4) Fitts's law (1954): ID = log2(2D/W), the one-bit
law (doubling W or halving D = exactly −1 bit), the screen-edge
observation (qualitative); (5) the design instincts — visibility,
feedback, mapping, affordance (Norman 1988; 'user experience' coined at
Apple, 1993), consistency; Nielsen's ten heuristics (1994, Molich–Nielsen
origin 1990) as the checklist instinct; (6) accessibility — the WCAG chain
(1.0: 5 May 1999; 2.0: 11 Dec 2008; 2.1: 5 Jun 2018; 2.2: 5 Oct 2023),
the widest-door instinct; (7) your body is part of the interface — the
20-20-20 rule (Anshel, late 1990s; AOA), posture honesty; (8) the
doorstep: Human-Computer Interaction (the shelf's later book).
