## The chapter arc (the WHY of the order)

The historian's method — what history is, the primary/secondary divide, the
external/internal criticism pair, the great historiographical lineage from
Herodotus to the Annales school (1.1) -> the sources themselves: the literary
register (religious, secular, foreign accounts) against the archaeological
register (epigraphy, numismatics, monuments, material culture) with the dating
sciences that give archaeology its numbers (1.2) -> time itself as the
historian's instrument: the BC/BCE-CE census and its no-year-zero arithmetic,
the calendar reforms (Julian 45 BCE, Gregorian 1582, the British 1752 jump),
the era systems (Saka, Vikram Samvat, Hijri), and periodisation as a CONVENTION
to be interrogated, not assumed — the Mill critique and the ancient/medieval/
modern triad (1.3). The arc hands off to ch.2 'The World Before 1200' (the AP
c. 1200 baseline).

### Lesson 1.1 (How History Is Written)
- Herodotus c. 484-c. 425 BCE (both 5th century BCE; 400-499 BCE), 'The
  Histories' on the Greco-Persian Wars, Cicero's 'Father of History', his stated
  aim 'to prevent the traces of human events from being erased by time'.
- Thucydides c. 460-c. 400 BCE, 'History of the Peloponnesian War', the
  eyewitness/exile method; Herodotus and Thucydides were coeval 35 years
  (460-425).
- Sima Qian c. 145-c. 86 BCE, Shiji completed c. 94 BCE, 130 chapters = 12
  annals + 10 tables + 8 treatises + 30 hereditary houses + 70 ordered
  biographies (sum 130), the taishi court-historian position, father of Chinese
  historiography.
- Ibn Khaldun 1332-1406, Muqaddimah 1377 (written in ~6 months at Qalat Ibn
  Salama), asabiyyah, father of historiography/sociology.
- Ranke 1795-1886 (91-year life), 'wie es eigentlich gewesen' (1824 preface);
  Carr 1892-1982 (69 at publication), 'What Is History?' 1961 Trevelyan
  Lectures, 'an unending dialogue between the present and the past';
  Bloch 1886-1944, Annales journal founded 1929 with Febvre (15 years before
  his death), 'The Historian's Craft' posthumous 1949 (5 years after).
- Herodotus death c. 425 BCE -> Sima Qian birth c. 145 BCE = 569 years apart
  (no-year-zero arithmetic); Herodotus c. 484 BCE -> Muqaddimah 1377 = 1860
  years.
- Definitions pinned: primary source (from the period itself: the witness,
  artifact, inscription, coin, document), secondary source (a later historian's
  account from the primaries); external criticism (is the evidence genuine —
  paper, script, language, anachronisms, provenance) vs internal criticism (is
  the testimony credible — bias, context, corroboration).

### Lesson 1.2 (Sources: Literary and Archaeological)
- Rigveda: 1028 hymns = 1017 + 11 valakhilya, 10 mandalas, oral transmission
  before writing; the literary register: religious literature, secular
  literature (court chronicles, biographies, drama), foreign accounts.
- Ashoka: 30+ inscriptions known, 14 Major Rock Edicts + 7 Major Pillar Edicts;
  pillars monolithic polished sandstone 12-15 m, up to 50 t; Brahmi deciphered
  by James Prinsep 1836-1838 (published March 1838), Devanampriya Piyadasi
  identified with Ashoka via George Turnour's Pali records; Kharoshthi in the
  northwest; Kandahar Greek-Aramaic bilingual + Greek-only edict.
- Rosetta Stone: Memphis decree of Ptolemy V, 27 March 196 BCE; found July
  1799 by Pierre-Francois Bouchard at Rashid (Rosetta) during Fort Julien
  repairs; hieroglyphic 14 lines + Demotic 32 + Greek 54 = 100 surviving lines;
  Champollion announced 1822 (23 years after the find); British Museum since
  1802 (3 years after); carved 196 BCE -> found 1799 = 1994 years in the ground.
- Foreign accounts: Megasthenes (ambassador of Seleucus I to Chandragupta
  Maurya, c. 302 BCE, 'Indica' surviving in fragments), Fa-Hien 399-414,
  Xuanzang 630-645 (15-year journeys each), Al-Biruni 973-1048 ('Kitab-ul-Hind'
  early 11th century); Megasthenes -> Fa-Hien = 700 years; Fa-Hien leaves 414 ->
  Xuanzang lands 630 = 216 years.
- Dating sciences: radiocarbon (half-life 5730 yr; after 1/2/3 half-lives =
  1/2, 1/4, 1/8; N = N0(1/2)^(t/5730); range ~50,000 yr; at the 50,000-yr edge
  0.236% remains; Libby mid-1940s University of Chicago, Nobel 1960); tree-ring
  dendrochronology; thermoluminescence; stratigraphy + the law of superposition
  (older below younger); the three-age system (Stone -> Bronze -> Iron, C.J.
  Thomsen 1836, Copenhagen); numismatics (punch-marked coins from c. 6th
  century BCE; Gupta gold dinars); Prayagraj Pillar inscription of Samudragupta
  composed by Harisena.
- Quiz key [2,3,1,0,2] = 23102.

### Lesson 1.3 (Time, Periodisation and the Calendar)
- BC/BCE and AD/CE: the AD era devised by Dionysius Exiguus in 525 CE (Easter
  table), popularized by Bede (8th century, 731); NO YEAR ZERO — 1 BCE is
  followed directly by 1 CE; the span rule: years from A BCE to B CE =
  A + B - 1. Witnesses: 1 BCE -> 1 CE = 1; Caesar 44 BCE -> Augustus 14 CE =
  57; Rosetta 196 BCE -> 1799 = 1994; Megasthenes 302 BCE -> Fa-Hien 399 = 700;
  476 CE -> 2026 = 1550.
- Centuries/millennia: 1st century CE = 1-100; 21st century = 2001-2100 (2026
  sits there and in the 3rd millennium); 3rd century BCE = 300-201 BCE; 2nd
  millennium BCE = 2000-1001 BCE; Herodotus c. 484 BCE = 5th century BCE.
- Calendars: Julian reform by Julius Caesar (46 BCE the 'year of confusion',
  effective 1 January 45 BCE; average 365.25 days); Gregorian reform by Pope
  Gregory XIII (bull Inter gravissimas, 1582; Thu 4 Oct -> Fri 15 Oct 1582, 10
  dates skipped; leap rule: divisible by 4, century years only if divisible by
  400 — 1600/2000 leap, 1700/1800/1900 not; average 365 + 97/400 = 365.2425 d;
  tropical year ~365.2422 d; Gregorian error ~0.0003 d/yr ~ 1 day in ~3,333
  years); Julian drift 0.0078 d/yr ~ 1 day in ~128 years; the 325-CE Nicaea
  reference: 325 -> 1582 = 1257 years ~ 9.8 = 10 days.
- Britain: Calendar (New Style) Act 1750; Wed 2 Sep -> Thu 14 Sep 1752, 11
  dates skipped (1582's 10 + 1 from 1700, the century year leap in Julian but
  not Gregorian); year begins 1 Jan from 1752 (was 25 March Lady Day); 'give
  us our eleven days'; Greece last European adopter 1923. Leap census:
  1901-2000 = 25 leap years (24 ordinary + 2000 quadricentennial); 2001-2100 =
  24 (2100 not leap).
- Era systems: Saka epoch 78 CE (Saka = CE - 78; 2026 -> Saka 1948; the Indian
  national calendar adopted 1957, Saha committee); Vikrama Samvat epoch 57 BCE
  (VS = CE + 57; spring 2026 -> VS 2083); Hijri epoch 622 CE, lunar year ~354
  days, drifts ~11 days/yr, 33 lunar years ~ 32 solar years (2026 -> Hijri
  ~1447/1448).
- Periodisation as convention: James Mill, 'The History of British India'
  (1817), the Hindu/Muslim/British register, never visited India, the
  religion-based critique (NCERT class 8 'How, When and Where'); 1817 -> 2026 =
  209 years; the ancient/medieval/modern triad with 476 CE (Romulus Augustulus
  deposed by Odoacer) and 1453 (fall of Constantinople) as conventional walls —
  the medieval band spans 977 years; the AP World c. 1200 baseline (the book's
  ch.2 title).
- Quiz key [0,2,3,1,0] = 02310.
